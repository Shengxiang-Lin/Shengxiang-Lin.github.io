abstract: >-
  Large language model (LLM)-enhanced recommender systems can exploit rich textual side information, but the resulting semantic representations may expose sensitive item attributes to inversion and re-identification attacks. We introduce relational fidelity as a privacy-utility objective: protected representations should move away from their original textual sources while preserving the relative semantic structure needed for recommendation. Based on this principle, PGPO constructs a prototype-aware semantic graph, learns privacy-preserving obfuscation anchors for structurally influential prototype words with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and progressively expands these mappings to the remaining vocabulary. Experiments on MovieLens-1M and Amazon-Book show that PGPO substantially reduces textual inversion risk while maintaining recommendation performance close to the unprotected upper bound.
